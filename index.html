01 / About

From microchips to the mathematics of learning

Who I am, why I changed course, and where I am going.

I came to İstanbul Technical University in 2020 to study Electronics and Communication Engineering, with a career in microchip design in mind. Four years and 169 ECTS later I made a deliberate lateral move into Mathematical Engineering. Hardware had taught me how computation is executed; I wanted to understand why the algorithms running on it work at all.

That question has driven everything since. Moving across was about unravelling the mathematics behind artificial intelligence: what a neural network is as a function, why gradient descent finds anything, how a language model turns statistics into fluency, and how we can tell, with stated confidence, whether one model really is better than another.

I am now in my seventh and final year at ITU, completing my last two terms and my graduation project. My electronics years left me with a systems view of CPUs, GPUs and memory hierarchies, which I bring to every algorithm I write.

Next, I want to dedicate myself to the mathematics of AI through a Master's and a PhD, and alongside that to start a research-driven venture or join a research team that designs the mathematical and architectural foundations of AI systems.

01

Every number has a script

No result in my reports is typed by hand. Each table, figure and quoted value is regenerated by one command from stored artifacts, and tests check the papers against them.

02

Protocols before results

Evaluation protocols, data splits and acceptance criteria are written down before the runs they govern; deviations are logged as dated decisions.

03

Proofs that compile

Where an argument can be formalised, I formalise it. certgen's formal statements are proved in Lean 4 with their axioms pinned at build time.

04

Negative results stay in

Experiment registries record failed and exploratory runs too, and my research repositories document their limitations.

05

AI as an instrument, me in the loop

I use large language models deliberately, as research and writing instruments under a human-in-the-loop verification workflow that checks every claim they help produce.

Alongside the projects above, I run a long-term personal and educational project: turning academics' lecture notes, handwritten pages and scattered material into structured digital documents typeset in LaTeX, for almost every course I have taken.

The work rests on prompt engineering and precise control of large language models. I have developed a pipeline that drives the models to produce publication-quality LaTeX with mathematical precision, and a human-in-the-loop review that checks every definition, derivation and example before a document is considered finished.
